Over the past several years, I have been fortunate to be part of a meaningful initiative: reaching out to students in rural government schools and sharing knowledge, experiences, and life lessons. What started as a small group of friends wanting to make a difference has grown into a remarkable journey of mentorship, learning, and community impact. Here, I share the story of how this all began and continues to evolve.

## What Does "Connecting with Students" Mean?

My first attempt at connecting with students of nearby schools came about in 2017. It all started with a bunch of friends who wanted to make this world a better place for as many families as possible by helping out the upcoming members of the families—the students. More than 15 like-minded people came together, all either school or college students.

With the combined efforts of my friends, family, and Mr. G. A. Ganesh, the founder and proprietor of HHH (Helping Hands to Humanity) trust, we drafted a 4-hour-long session wherein we talked about our experiences and methods of learning, conducted mini quizzes and competitions, discussed our choices of careers and how we hoped to pursue them. That was the start of a remarkable journey. We conducted almost 10 such events within the span of two years.

## The Origins of the Movement

At school, I discovered that I like teaching my peers and from their feedback and progress, that I am also good at it. My family had always insisted on the importance of knowledge and how it can shape one's life. When I realized that my friends also wanted to help others learn, things took a quick turn and we became an independent group of people willing to work after hours to make this a possibility.

During my 10th grade, we had finally gathered enough knowledge and contacts to reach out to other government schools. As 10th graders, we ran the risk of not being taken seriously. So for our first attempt, we chose the classes 6, 7, 8 of KGBV Government Residential School in Denkanikottai. It was a successful event! Within the next two years, we had conducted over 10 events in different schools and orphanages across the Hosur region.

### Sessions and Activities

We talked about lifestyle, the importance of education, health, family, good virtues, and reading. We taught students mnemonics and other memory techniques. We guided students in their career choices. We also helped them deal with their personal and family problems.

Our events usually included discussions with students about methods of learning and the importance of education; quizzes and competitions to find talented individuals and direct them toward their potential; and singing and dancing for relaxation. We distributed a pen to each student who participated as a token of gratitude.

![Image 2: Student Engagement Activity](/assets/images/posts/connecting-with-students/activity-1.jpg)

## Our Plan and Approach

We tried to come up with interactive sessions involving games, activities, and discussions. This was harder than we anticipated. To provide informative, engaging yet concise sessions to students who were almost the same age as we were required careful planning.

Keeping in mind their attention span and the kind of cross-perspective we wanted to provide, we drafted our sessions to include:

**Personal narratives emphasizing:**
- Decision making
- Good virtues
- Life skills
- Learning vs Understanding
- Overcoming setbacks and failures
- Career options

**Student activities promoting:**
- Public speaking
- Art and creativity
- Decision making
- Reasoning and verbal ability

Initially, our sessions took as long as 4 to 5 hours. But upon feedback from teachers and students, we brought it down to less than 3 hours. We could not document the feedbacks formally as all funding for the sessions was spared by group members who were of the non-working class. However, we took the feedback as word of mouth and constantly improved. After every session, students were asked if they felt that they enjoyed and appreciated what was told. The teachers and students have always treated us with great kindness and hospitality.

![Image 3: Large Group Session](/assets/images/posts/connecting-with-students/session-2.jpg)

## The Transition and Continuation

Although we were very successful in those initial two years, we had to dissolve the group after schooling as everyone moved to different places for college. Since I got back home from college due to the pandemic, I had been waiting for schools to reopen so I could go back to connecting with students. I jumped at the chance and conducted three sessions on lifestyle and philosophy during the fall of 2021.

Upon writing my first blog about these experiences, I called a few of my old friends and asked if they were still interested in conducting more such events. Some of the old group mates had come back home due to the pandemic and were happy to jump at the opportunity to meet refreshing, young minds. Now that we had completed schooling, I felt we were more suited to offer career guidance and learning advice to our brothers and sisters in rural schools around Hosur.

![Image 4: Recent Session with Volunteers](/assets/images/posts/connecting-with-students/session-3.jpg)

## A Recent Event: July 2022

With heart-filled joy and excitement, Mr. G. A. Ganesh arranged for my friends and I to visit Onnalvadi High School again. My school friends T. G. Abhishekk and H. Haarish Infant Raj, and my mother, N. Hema, joined me in connecting with the students. Abhishekk had completed his final year in B.Sc. Microbiology, and Haarish was in the final phase of his B.Sc. Computer Science.

The afternoon we went, about a hundred students were sitting in a compact room ready to lend their ears to us. They were mostly 8th and 9th-grade students. To my surprise, a few of them recognized me—evidence of the lasting impact of previous sessions.

This event was important to all of us as we had decided to share the very things that we wished we'd have liked to hear when we were of their age.

My mom shared her experience about how good thoughts and good deeds positively impact our lives. Haarish explained some mysteries about how websites work technically and what's the best way to utilize them. Abhishekk encouraged them with a story about knowledge and character and how it shapes one's life. I was keen on sharing my experience with public speaking and the way to do it with as little performance anxiety as possible.

We also wanted to give them practical exposure to public speaking. So, we asked two of them to volunteer and talk for a minute about anything they wanted to. Whilst they found it hard to come out of their fears, they were all eager and willing to try. I was very happy to see that with help, almost every one of them was willing to give their best to become better versions of themselves.

![Image 5: Interactive Session Moment](/assets/images/posts/connecting-with-students/session-4.jpg)

## The Journey Continues

I have now conducted multiple sessions across different schools and continue to be part of this movement. The impact goes beyond academics—it touches the lives of students in ways that formal education alone cannot. It's about inspiring them to think bigger, to believe in themselves, and to understand that knowledge paired with good character can truly shape their futures.

As I pursue my PhD in Computational and Data Sciences, this work remains close to my heart. I am grateful to all the teachers, students, and coordinators who have made this possible and continue to make things easy for us to connect with the younger generations of India.

If you are interested in learning more about these sessions, mentorship opportunities, or would like to organize similar events in your community, I would be delighted to hear from you.
